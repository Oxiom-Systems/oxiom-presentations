02 · Strongest evidence

Evidence changes the conversation.

01 · Fact Put the most decision-relevant proof first.

Use a metric, observation, quote, screenshot, or accepted constraint from the source set.

02 · Meaning Explain what the evidence changes.

Connect the fact to risk, priority, value, readiness, or the question the audience must answer.

03 · Boundary Keep the source's qualification visible.

Do not turn a target, estimate, proposal, or illustration into an established outcome.

Turn the strongest source material into two or three facts the audience can understand at a glance, then explain why those facts matter. [Sources] STARTER-01 in sources/source-ledger.txt; replace each fact with its supporting source identifier.

03 · Interpretation

Make the gap explicit.

Use this split to separate the conclusion from the proof, trade-offs, or implications that support it.

  1. 01What is working or known now
  2. 02What remains uncertain or unproven
  3. 03What evidence would close the gap
Use an editorial split when one side should carry the conclusion and the other should carry the proof or implications. Preserve every material qualification from the source. [Sources] STARTER-01 in sources/source-ledger.txt; replace with the sources for the current state, gap, and acceptance evidence.

04 · Controlled path

A gated path makes progress credible.

Every stage earns the next through named evidence, ownership, or acceptance.

01UnderstandEstablish the real baseline

02ProveTest the uncertain link

03DecideScale, redesign, or stop

The next move follows the evidence, not momentum.

Show only the stages that matter to the decision in the room. Every gate should name the proof, owner, or acceptance condition that lets the work move forward. [Sources] STARTER-01 in sources/source-ledger.txt; replace with the programme, roadmap, or decision-gate sources.
